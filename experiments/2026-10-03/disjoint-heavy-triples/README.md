# Conditional bound

Let a full C(16,5,3) covering contain q vertex-disjoint triples, each appearing
in at least seven blocks, where 0 <= q <= 5. If the covering has b blocks,
then its total pair excess is at least

    9q - floor(q/2),

and consequently

    b >= ceil((600 + 9q - floor(q/2))/10).

In particular, five such triples require at least 65 blocks. This statement
does not assume regular point degrees. It excludes this specific structure
from a 64-block cover; it is not an unconditional lower bound of 65 for
C(16,5,3), and no novelty claim is made.

# Proof

Write lambda(x,y) for the number of blocks containing a pair. Covering all
fourteen triples extending that pair requires lambda(x,y) >= ceil(14/3) = 5.
There are 120 pairs and each block contains ten pairs. Therefore the total
nonnegative pair excess is

    E = sum over pairs (lambda - 5) = 10b - 600.

The three internal pairs of each of the q disjoint heavy triples have
multiplicity at least seven. Their 3q distinct edges therefore contribute
at least 6q to E.

For each heavy triple T, choose any seven of its containing blocks. Each has
two points outside T, so there are fourteen outside incidences on only
thirteen outside points. Some outside point d_T occurs in at least two of
the chosen blocks. For any a in T, the pair {a,d_T} appears with each of the
two other points of T at least twice. The fourteen possible third points
must all occur, while these two third points each contribute at least one
additional occurrence. Thus

    3 lambda(a,d_T) >= 14 + 2 = 16,

which implies lambda(a,d_T) >= 6. The three hub edges from T to d_T therefore
each contribute at least one unit of excess.

There are 3q hub-edge occurrences. None is an internal edge of one of the
chosen triples: one endpoint belongs to T and the other lies outside T,
while the chosen triples are disjoint. Within one triple the three edges are
different. If an edge is generated by two different triples T_i and T_j,
then necessarily d_i belongs to T_j, d_j belongs to T_i, and that repeated
edge is exactly {d_i,d_j}. A hub edge cannot occur for three triples because
it has only two endpoints.

Direct each triple towards the chosen triple containing its hub, if there
is one. This directed graph has outdegree at most one. Duplicated hub edges
correspond exactly to reciprocal directed pairs, which are disjoint
two-cycles. There are at most floor(q/2) of these. Hence there are at least
3q - floor(q/2) distinct hub edges, disjoint from the 3q internal pairs.

Combining the two sets of excess contributions gives

    E >= 6q + 3q - floor(q/2) = 9q - floor(q/2).

Together with E = 10b - 600 this proves the stated bound. For q = 5 the
right side is 43, exceeding the 40 available excess units of a 64-block
cover. Repetition of a hub outside all selected triples, or at the same
point of another triple, creates no additional duplicate edges and does
not invalidate the count.

# Finite counting audit

`check_hubs.py` separately enumerates every hub assignment on five fixed
disjoint triples. Relabeling permits this fixed choice without loss of
generality. For each q from zero through five it examines all 13^q allowed
outside-hub assignments, directly constructs the union of hub edges, and
checks the lower bound 3q - floor(q/2). It also checks that the hub-edge
union avoids every internal pair. This finite audit supplements the proof;
the incidence and pair-covering steps are justified in the proof above.

uv run python experiments/2026-10-03/disjoint-heavy-triples/check_hubs.py
